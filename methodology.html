The ranking, without the mystery

A good price only matters
when the game is worth playing.

LootRadar’s Deal Score is a ranking aid, not a review score. It combines signals from the current listing and available game metadata; a giant discount cannot rescue a poorly reviewed game.

Deal ScoreQuality × 35% + Price value × 25% + Discount × 20% + Review confidence × 10% + Player interest × 10% − penalties

The five signals

What goes into the score

35%

Quality

Steam user sentiment carries most of this signal, with critic score contributing when available. One source can stand alone, but missing information is never disguised as certainty.

25%

Price value

When a user opens a game, its current price is compared with CheapShark’s recorded historical low. In the list view, CheapShark Deal Rating is used as a clearly labeled proxy.

20%

Discount strength

The discount contribution tops out at 70% off. A louder badge does not earn more points after that.

10%

Review confidence

Review count is scaled logarithmically. Moving from 10 to 1,000 reviews matters much more than moving from 100,000 to 110,000.

10%

Player interest

Review volume is combined with a modest release-recency signal. Popularity can support a recommendation; it cannot replace quality.

Default quality gate

What you see first

The main feed normally requires at least 70% positive user reviews and 100 reviews when those values are available, plus a meaningful discount or strong price-value signal. Missing critic data does not automatically hide a game.

Filters let you widen review, price, and content thresholds. Curated collections may keep stricter rules of their own. Filter choices are written into the URL so a result can be bookmarked or shared.

Excluded by default

  • DLC and season passes
  • Demos, prologues, and playtests
  • Soundtracks and art books
  • Virtual currency and cosmetic packs
  • Bundles and duplicate listings
  • Spam-like titles and suspicious list prices
  • Lower-confidence Early Access listings

Sources & limits

What the score can—and cannot—say

CheapShark supplies current store and price listings. Steam and SteamSpy metadata may add review, popularity, and game-detail context. LootRadar applies its own eligibility rules and Deal Score to those inputs.

The published snapshot refreshes every three hours. Prices, availability, and metadata can change between snapshots, and the retailer’s checkout page controls the final amount.

LootRadar does not currently have a licensed full price time series, dependable bundle-frequency history, console pricing, publisher batch-release analysis, or verified Steam Deck status. Missing information lowers confidence; it is not filled in with a guess.

Some outbound links may be affiliate links. Affiliate relationships never affect the score, eligibility, or ranking order.
