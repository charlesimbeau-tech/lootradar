Inexpensive Steam games worth playing

Reviewed July 2026 · LootRadar guide

Steam is drowning in cheap games. That part is easy. The hard part is working out which cheap games are still generous with your time once the price badge stops being the interesting thing about them.

Everything below regularly falls into single digits during a sale, though prices shift with the date, the region, the retailer, and the edition. Use these as shopping context, then go check what the listing actually says today.

The reliable single-digit heroes

  • Celeste: Razor-precise platforming, an accessibility menu other studios should be copying, and a story that turns the difficulty into the point rather than an obstacle.
  • Undertale: A little RPG that quietly watches what kind of player you are and then has opinions about it. Worth going in unspoiled, even now.
  • Terraria: Dig, build, fight, repeat, for approximately the rest of your life. Absurd value at any price it has ever been sold for.
  • FTL: Faster Than Light: Command a ship you cannot quite afford to repair. Every run ends in a story you will tell someone about later.
  • Into the Breach: Tiny maps, telegraphed enemy moves, and turns that feel like disarming a bomb with tweezers. Perfect design, not one wasted button.

These make good sale targets because the designs held up, not because some article from 2019 attached a number to them.

Action games that never went stale

  • Hades: The one that made dying feel like progress. The combat is superb and the writing gives every failed escape a new conversation to look forward to.
  • Dead Cells: Fast, mean, and enormously satisfying once the rhythm clicks. Repetition here feels like practice, not paperwork.
  • Vampire Survivors: You only move. Somehow that becomes a screen-filling firework show and a genuinely deep build puzzle. Costs less than a coffee.
  • Slay the Spire: Understated presentation, monstrous strategic depth. Every card you take changes the odds of everything after it.

Check what Steam says today before treating last month’s price as a fact.

Big games that eventually get cheap

  • Doom (2016): The one that decided cover shooting was for cowards and made running directly at the demons the correct tactic. Still exhilarating.
  • Dishonored: Dense, generous levels that reward improvisation and let you leave an entire palace guard asleep in various cupboards. Every playthrough looks different.
  • The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt: Hundreds of hours of quests that treat side characters like people. Just be careful comparing the base game against the Complete edition, because the price gap is not always the content gap.
  • BioShock: The Collection: Three story-led shooters with settings you will still be describing to people years later. Check which remastered and original versions are actually in the box.
  • Portal and Portal 2: Still sharper, funnier and better paced than most things released last year. If somehow you have not played them, this is your sign.

Worth waiting for a proper discount

  • Outer Wilds: A solar system on a 22-minute clock where the only thing you collect is knowledge. Do not read anything about it. Seriously, close the wiki.
  • Disco Elysium: A detective RPG where your own skills argue with you in your head, and failing a check often produces the better scene. Some of the finest writing in the medium.
  • Inscryption: Starts as a card game in a cabin. Does not stay that. Buy it, play it, and tell no one what happens.
  • Subnautica: Beautiful, tense, and quietly terrifying once you realize how far down the map goes. Not a checklist ocean.

Check what you are buying, not just what it is called

A tempting price can belong to the wrong edition, a DLC pack pretending to be a game, or a key that activates on a launcher you were not expecting. Match the product, region, launcher, and included content before you pay. Bundles need the closest look of all, since a generous-looking package often contains two games you already own and a soundtrack.

Review confidence deserves the same scrutiny. A shiny 96% built on 40 reviews tells you far less than 88% across 40,000, and recent reviews are where you will find out whether a well-loved game has started crashing on modern hardware.
