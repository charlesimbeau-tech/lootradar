Free PC games worth knowing about

Reviewed July 2026 · LootRadar guide

Free can mean a game that is permanently free, a giveaway that ends Thursday, or a shop with a game attached. Those are three different deals wearing the same price tag. Below are the ones that give you a genuinely substantial game for nothing, sorted by what you are actually signing up for.

Monetization, account requirements, and availability all change, sometimes overnight. Check the official store page before you install, and decide in advance which kinds of progression systems and purchase prompts you are willing to have in your life.

A price of zero can mean a limited giveaway or a free-to-play game. Read the store terms before you claim or install anything.

Shooters that cost nothing

  • Counter-Strike 2: Twenty-five years of refinement into movement, map knowledge, and team economy. The skill ceiling is somewhere in orbit, and that is the appeal.
  • Valorant: Tactical rounds plus character abilities, so a well-timed smoke matters as much as your aim. Punishing solo, brilliant with a squad who talk.
  • Apex Legends: The battle royale where the movement is the real weapon. Sliding down a hill while reloading still feels fantastic, win or lose.
  • The Finals: Objectives inside arenas you can demolish. Sometimes the winning play is not the firefight, it is removing the floor the enemy team is standing on.

Competitive games move fast, so before you commit a hundred hours check the current player numbers, your region’s server support, what anti-cheat it installs, and what exactly is being sold in the shop.

Action RPGs and long-haul grinds

  • Path of Exile: A skill tree the size of a national rail map and a build community that treats theorycrafting as a competitive sport. Complexity is the feature, not the bug.
  • Warframe: Fast co-op action wrapped around a decade of missions and gear. It will not explain itself for the first ten hours. Push through and it becomes one of the most generous free games out there.
  • Genshin Impact: Genuinely lovely open-world exploration attached to a randomized character system. Set your spending limit before you start, not after you see a banner you want.

A price of zero does not make your time or your attention free. Look at what progression items are sold, how hard the game nags you to log in daily, and whether the core loop is still fun for someone who never spends a penny.

Pick the game for the game. Then read the business model carefully.

Strategy, MOBAs, and card games

  • Dota 2: Famously the deep end of the pool. Drafting, positioning, timing, and item choices combine into something genuinely intimidating and genuinely brilliant, with every hero unlocked from day one.
  • League of Legends: Easier to read than Dota, which is not the same as simple. Enormous roster, heavy focus on lane control, and a community with opinions.
  • Marvel Snap: Six turns, simultaneous reveals, and the constant question of whether to double down or run. Perfect for the queue at the post office.

Card and character games often tie variety to how much of the collection you have unlocked. Check how you get new pieces before deciding whether free leaves you enough room to play your way.

MMOs you can walk into for nothing

  • Guild Wars 2: No subscription, a huge core world, and public events that pull strangers into the same fight without anyone having to organize anything. Expansions cost money, the base game does not.
  • Final Fantasy XIV Free Trial: The trial famously includes an entire base game and an expansion, with no limit on playtime. Social and account restrictions apply, so read the current terms.
  • Star Wars: The Old Republic: Eight class stories that hold up as a solo BioWare RPG, which is not what most people expect from a free MMO. Convenience features are gated by account status.

Giveaways with a countdown on them

Epic, Steam, GOG, and various publishers regularly hand out paid games for nothing. These do not belong in an evergreen list, because the game and the window will both have changed before any article catches up.

Three things to confirm on the official page every time: do you keep it, when does the claim window close, and which account or launcher ends up holding the license. And if it is a Prime Gaming offer, remember that is a perk of a subscription you pay for, not a free game.

Building a free library you will actually open

  1. Claim things because you want to play them, not because the button is briefly exciting.
  2. Pick one or two free-to-play games. Six competing daily login routines is a part-time job.
  3. Decide your spending limit before you walk into a store designed around optional purchases.
  4. Check the account, launcher, region, and ownership terms on the official page.
  5. Uninstall what you do not play. Your SSD is allowed to have standards.

Free is a price, not a verdict. Worth playing is still the test.
