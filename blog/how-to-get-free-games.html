How to get free PC games legally

Reviewed July 2026 · LootRadar guide

“Free” is doing an enormous amount of work in PC gaming. It can mean a game you claim and own forever, a game funded by optional purchases you will be reminded about constantly, a trial that evaporates on Sunday night, or a title included with a membership you already pay for. The price says zero in all four cases. What you actually end up owning is wildly different.

Stick to official storefronts, publisher pages, and communities with a track record. Before anything reaches your drive, know who is handing it to you, which account or launcher it drags along, and whether your access dies when the promotion does.

Epic Games Store giveaways

Epic has been doing this for years now: limited-time games you can add to your account without paying. Which game, how long you have, which regions qualify, and the exact terms all shift, so check the store’s own free-games section rather than a screenshot someone posted.

Claim inside the window and the game normally lands in your library for keeps. Normally. Read the listing anyway, because a free weekend and a trial press the same cheerful zero-dollar button.

Amazon Prime Gaming

Prime Gaming hands out games and in-game items as a perk of an active Amazon Prime membership. Which makes it a benefit of something you pay for, not a free service. Worth being precise about that when you are working out whether Prime is worth it.

Delivery is inconsistent. Some titles live in the Amazon Games app, some arrive as a key for another storefront. Two things to check before you install yet another launcher: is there an activation deadline, and does the game stay yours if the membership lapses?

Steam promotions

Steam runs at least four different things that all look free at a glance: permanently free games, limited “free to keep” promotions, demos, and free weekends. Read the button. “Play for free” often means your access ends Monday morning. “Add to account” usually means it is genuinely yours.

Publisher giveaways here are short and unpredictable. Let the Steam store page be the truth, and treat any social post that skips the end time or routes you through a domain you have never seen with the suspicion it has earned.

GOG giveaways

GOG runs limited claims during store promotions too. It is the DRM-free storefront, which is great, but the terms of each individual offer still matter. Confirm the offer adds the full game to your library, and write down the deadline.

GOG also keeps a handful of classics permanently free, no countdown attached. Those are the best kind, because you can think about it for a week and lose nothing.

A price of zero is where the checking starts, not where it stops.

itch.io and going straight to the developer

itch.io is a glorious mess of commercial releases, free downloads, pay-what-you-want experiments, demos, prototypes, and game-jam oddities made in 48 caffeinated hours. The range is the whole point, and it is where a lot of genuinely strange, genuinely brilliant stuff lives. It also means you read each project page rather than assuming the storefront works one way.

Developers and publishers hand out free games, open betas, and older releases from their own sites too. Use the official domain or a verified store page, and remember that a download mirror posted by a stranger does not become trustworthy because the thumbnail is exciting.

Promotions, betas, and other things that are not ownership

Studios run promotions around anniversaries, big updates, and launches. Open betas hand you an unfinished game for a weekend. Neither is the same as owning the finished thing, however good the trailer was.

  • Giveaway: check the claim deadline and whether the license actually stays in your account.
  • Free weekend: access ends. Your save might carry over into a purchase, but confirm that on the store page first.
  • Open beta: expect broken things, wiped progress, and a server that goes away.
  • Demo: expect a carefully chosen slice, cut off at the most tempting possible moment.

Free-to-play, and what it costs

Free-to-play games charge nothing at the door and then sell cosmetics, characters, inventory slots, convenience, and the occasional randomized box. The useful question is not whether you can avoid spending. It is whether the game is still fun for someone who never does.

Before you sink a weekend in, look at how progression is paced, which purchases touch the actual gameplay, whether the parental controls suit your household, and whether it wants a whole separate account. Our guide to free PC games worth knowing about sorts the genuinely substantial ones from the countdown-timer claims.

Subscriptions are not free, they are prepaid

Game Pass, Humble Choice, Prime Gaming and friends all include games, and all of them cost money. Catalog access ends when you stop paying or when a title rotates out, which it will, usually the week you finally start it. Some perks hand you a permanent key. Most just rent you the shelf.

None of that makes them bad value. “Included with something I already pay for” can be a genuinely great deal. It is just a different sentence from “free,” and it is worth saying the accurate one.

The sixty-second sanity check

  1. Open the official store, publisher, or developer page. Not the link from the tweet.
  2. Confirm the exact game, edition, region, and activation platform.
  3. Work out whether this is permanent, time-limited, or tied to a membership.
  4. Note the claim deadline and any separate activation deadline.
  5. Walk away from anything that wants your antivirus off or ships its own mystery installer.
  6. Remember a free game still costs you disk space, attention, and an evening.
